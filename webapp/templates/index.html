Drop in a brain MRI. Get a four-way read.

A MobileNetV2 model sorts each slice into glioma, meningioma, pituitary macroadenoma or healthy, and shows how confident it is in every class.

No scan handy? Try one from the test set.

86.9% accuracy on 229 scans the model never saw. For research and education only, not a medical device.

Your result appears here: the predicted class and a probability for every class.

How it works

  1. 1

    Upload

    Choose a brain MRI slice (JPG or PNG). It is sent only to the server running on your own machine.

  2. 2

    Preprocess

    The image is resized to 224 x 224 and converted to RGB. Raw 0-255 pixels go straight to the model, which rescales them itself.

  3. 3

    Classify

    MobileNetV2 features feed global average pooling, dropout and a softmax output across the four classes.

  4. 4

    Read the result

    You get the predicted class plus a probability for every class, so low-confidence cases are easy to spot.

What it recognizes

Glioma

Tumors that arise from glial cells in the brain or spinal cord. Often infiltrative with irregular borders.

Meningioma

Usually slow-growing tumors of the meninges, the membranes around the brain. Typically well-defined and attached to the dura.

Pituitary Macroadenoma

Tumors of the pituitary gland larger than 10 mm, located at the base of the brain near the sella turcica.

Healthy

MRI slices with no visible tumor, used so the model also learns what normal anatomy looks like.

Model and data

Architecture

  • MobileNetV2 base pretrained on ImageNet, input 224 x 224 x 3
  • Rescaling is part of the model, so the app sends raw pixels
  • Global average pooling, Dropout 0.35, softmax over 4 classes
  • Phase 1: train the head with the base frozen, Adam 1e-3
  • Phase 2: unfreeze the top of the base, Adam 1e-4, early stopping on validation loss

Dataset

  • Multi-Class Brain Tumor MRI Dataset, Mendeley Data (CC BY 4.0)
  • 1,137 images: 908 training, 229 testing, stratified 80/20
  • 15% of training held out for validation
  • Augmentation: horizontal flip, rotation, zoom, brightness and contrast
  • Evaluated with classification report, confusion matrix and per-class ROC/AUC

Important

NeuroScan is a student research project. It is not a medical device and must not be used to diagnose, treat or rule out any condition. Any MRI finding needs review by a qualified radiologist or physician.

NeuroScan, a MobileNetV2 classifier built with TensorFlow, Keras, Flask and Bootstrap. Dataset: Jamil and Khan (2025), Mendeley Data, doi:10.17632/82mtzd8x72.2. Sample scans come from the dataset's test split.
